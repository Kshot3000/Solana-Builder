# Solana Ecosystem Builder

An independent builder hub for the whole Solana ecosystem — tools, a verified project catalogue, and guides, by Kyle Cox ([@kshot9000](https://x.com/kshot9000), [github.com/Kshot3000](https://github.com/Kshot3000)).

**Live hub:** https://kshot3000.github.io/Solana-Builder/

Raydium-specific work also lives at [Raydium Renaissance](https://kshot3000.github.io/Raydium-Renaissance/); my Solana Pay tool is the [Link Desk](https://kshot3000.github.io/solana-pay-link-desk/).

## Tools (all educational models, browser-local)

1. **SOL ⇄ lamports converter** — exact BigInt conversion; 1 SOL = 1,000,000,000 lamports, no rounding.
2. **Constant-product swap model** — x·y=k with the fee on the input and price impact, exact scaled-BigInt math.
3. **Rent-exempt minimum calculator** — (128 + dataLen) × 3,480 × 2 lamports, the network's rent parameters.
4. **Impermanent-loss calculator** — 2√r/(1+r) − 1 for a 50/50 pool position.
5. **Transaction fee estimator** — 5,000 lamports per signature plus the priority fee, ceil(CU limit × CU price in micro-lamports ÷ 1,000,000), charged on the requested CU limit (capped at 1,400,000).
6. **SPL token amount converter** — UI amount ⇄ raw units by the mint's own decimals (USDC 6, SOL 9, some mints 0), exact BigInt; amounts finer than the mint's decimals are rejected, never rounded.
7. **Solana address checker** — base58 format validation: every character in the base58 alphabet (no 0, O, I or l) and a decoded length of exactly 32 bytes; format only — it does not check that an account exists or is safe to send to.
8. **Staking rewards estimator** — rewards once per epoch (432,000 slots, ~2 days, ~182.5 epochs/year), validator commission taken from the reward, net rewards compounded epoch by epoch; the annual yield is a user assumption, not a live network yield.
9. **Solana Pay URL builder & inspector** — builds and parses transfer-request URLs (`solana:<recipient>?amount=…&spl-token=…&reference=…&label=…&message=…&memo=…`) per the Solana Pay specification: the recipient must be a native account (associated token accounts must not be used), amounts are user units with the asset's decimal precision enforced where the mint is known locally (SOL 9, USDC and USDG 6), duplicate single-value fields and malformed percent-encoding are rejected, and nothing is sent.
10. **CLMM tick ⇄ price converter & range checker** — concentrated liquidity (Raydium CLMM, Orca Whirlpools): price(tick) = 1.0001^tick over the on-chain tick range −443,636 to 443,636, price-to-tick as a floor, and a position-range check that enforces endpoint alignment to the pool's tick spacing and reports whether the current tick is in range; prices are in raw token units.
11. **Slippage tolerance & minimum-received calculator** — the bound every DEX interface signs: minimum received = expected out × (1 − tolerance) and maximum input = expected in × (1 + tolerance), both floored in exact scaled-BigInt so the bound is never looser than the tolerance picked (an expected 4 at 0.5% protects at 3.98); a dust quote whose minimum floors to 0 is flagged as protecting nothing.
12. **Epoch schedule converter** — slot ⇄ epoch using the runtime's real EpochSchedule, warmup included: epochs 0–13 double from 32 slots, the first normal epoch is 14 at slot 524,256, and only then does an epoch hold a constant 432,000 slots — so the textbook `slot ÷ 432,000` is a dozen-plus epochs behind. Schedule math in slots only; no wall-clock conversion, because the target slot duration has changed repeatedly.
13. **Token-2022 transfer fee calculator** — the fee a transfer-fee mint withholds on every transfer: fee = ceil(raw amount × basis points ÷ 10,000), capped at the mint's maximum fee, computed in exact BigInt on raw units, plus the inverse gross-up (the smallest amount to send so the recipient receives an exact net). The ceiling means any positive transfer at a positive rate pays at least 1 raw unit; the classic SPL Token program has no transfer fees.
14. **Sandwich attack (MEV) model** — the canonical MEV extraction on a constant-product pool, with all three legs (front-run, victim fill, back-run) run through Tool 2's own swap math against the reserves each leg leaves: at a loose victim slippage tolerance the attacker's signed profit is the victim's loss; at a tight tolerance the victim's transaction aborts at its minimum-received bound and the attacker loses the round-trip costs unwinding alone.
15. **Liquid staking (LST) exchange-rate calculator** — a stake pool's token priced as total staked SOL ÷ token supply: deposits mint and redemptions return at that rate in exact floored BigInt, and a user-supplied rewards amount shows why the rate rises while holder balances stand still (the same redemption returns more SOL; the same deposit mints fewer tokens). No pool fees and no unstake delay modelled, and both omissions are stated.
16. **Program-derived address (PDA) finder** — the full derivation in the browser: SHA-256 over seeds ‖ bump ‖ program ID ‖ "ProgramDerivedAddress", with the bump scanned 255 downward for the first hash that lands off the ed25519 curve (checked with BigInt field math, including the negative-zero encoding edge), enforcing the runtime's limits of 16 seeds × 32 bytes, text seeds as their UTF-8 bytes. Cross-verified against the reference SDKs, including a real associated-token-account derivation.
17. **CLMM position amounts calculator** — what a concentrated-liquidity position actually holds: from a liquidity amount L and a tick range, the token0/token1 amounts in all three branches (inside the range, only token0 below it, only token1 above it), using the shared square-root-price CLMM formula cross-verified against a reference SDK's Q96 integer implementation. Principal only — earned fees accrue separately and are stated as excluded.
18. **LP deposit & pool share calculator** — what a constant-product deposit mints: supply × min(depositA ÷ reserveA, depositB ÷ reserveB), floored exact BigInt, with the used/excess split per side, the limiting token, and the depositor's share afterwards; the first deposit into an empty pool mints the geometric mean sqrt(depositA × depositB) by exact integer square root. A pool-specific first-mint minimum lock is stated as not modelled.
19. **Token vesting calculator** — cliff + linear unlock: accrual is linear over the full duration from day one (total × elapsed ÷ duration, floored exact BigInt at the smallest unit) but nothing is claimable before the cliff month, when the whole accrued share releases at once; reports accrued, claimable, locked, the at-cliff release and months remaining. Whole months only, and the minority accrue-from-the-cliff convention is stated as not this model.
20. **Lending health & liquidation calculator** — a borrow position judged the way Solana lending markets judge it: collateral value = amount × price, LTV = debt ÷ value, health factor = (value × liquidation threshold) ÷ debt, max borrow total = value × max LTV, and the liquidation price = debt ÷ (amount × threshold), in exact scaled BigInt (headline: health 1.6, liquidation price 62.5 on 10 at 100 against 500 of debt at an 80% threshold). Single collateral and debt, no interest accrual and no liquidation bonus or fee, stated as not modelled.
21. **Transaction size & packet-limit calculator** — a serialized transaction measured exactly against the 1,232-byte packet limit: shortvec count + 64 bytes per signature, 3-byte header, shortvec + 32 bytes per static key, 32-byte blockhash, and one instruction's index/count/indices/data; a plain SOL transfer is exactly 215 bytes. In v0 form, accounts loaded through one address lookup table cost a 1-byte index each — but the table reference costs 32-plus bytes first, so a single table account makes the transaction 4 bytes bigger while the headline transfer with two table accounts saves 27 bytes (188 total). One instruction and one table modelled, stated on the page.

22. **Anchor account space planner** — an Anchor account sized exactly: 8 discriminator bytes plus Borsh field bytes packed with no padding (bool/u8 1, u16 2, u32 4, u64 8, u128 16, Pubkey 32; String/Vec<u8> 4 + capacity, Vec 4 + count × element, array count × element, Option 1 + inner), priced with the rent formula because space is the deposit: a vault struct of Pubkey + u64 + bool + String(32) is 85 bytes locking 1,482,480 lamports. Four fields modelled, enums stated as not modelled (1 + largest variant, by hand).
23. **Sale split calculator** — how a marketplace sale or escrow release divides one price: platform fee and creator royalty, each floored at the smallest unit in exact BigInt (price × bps ÷ 10,000), and the seller as the remainder, so the three parts always sum to the price exactly and the seller keeps the flooring dust (100 at 200/500 bps pays 2 / 5 / 93; a 3-unit price split 50/50 pays 1 / 1 / 1). Rates summing past 10,000 bps are rejected; both cuts are taken out of the price and royalty enforcement limits are stated, not modelled.
24. **Stake activation & cooldown timer** — when delegated stake actually becomes effective (or withdrawable): at each epoch boundary an account may change by its portion ÷ the total moving the same way × the cluster's previous-epoch effective stake × the warmup/cooldown rate — 900 bps (9%) on the current network, 2,500 bps before the rate reduction — floored in exact BigInt, clamped to its own portion, with the program's 1-lamport minimum progress per epoch (40,000,000 SOL solo against a 400,000,000 SOL cluster: 36,000,000 in epoch 1, done in epoch 2; a 10× crowd stretches a 10,000,000 SOL move from 1 epoch to 3). The cluster's effective stake is held constant at the user's figure and the assumption is stated on the page.
25. **CLMM uncollected fees calculator** — what a concentrated-liquidity position can collect, computed the way the pool computes it: accrued = floor((fee growth inside now − fee growth inside at last touch) × liquidity ÷ 2^64), the subtraction wrapping modulo 2^128 exactly like the on-chain u128 counters (Raydium CLMM's X64 fee-growth layout), plus the tokens already settled as owed by an earlier touch; all in exact BigInt on the raw integer counters, with the total also shown at each token's decimals (growth up exactly 2^64 at liquidity 1,000,000 accrues exactly 1,000,000 raw units). The counters are user inputs, as an explorer or the SDK reports them — no live position is read.
26. **Inflation schedule calculator** — how fast new SOL is issued: rate = max(1.5%, 8% × (1 − disinflation)^years), the validator's schedule with a fractional year (it glides per epoch, from the epoch-150 start), the disinflation rate an input defaulting to the shipped 1,500 bps (15%) so the governance-adopted-but-unimplemented 30% can be modelled without being presented as live; annual issuance is the rate × a user supply held constant, stated on the page (at year 5: 3.5496%, issuing 17,748,212.5 SOL on a 500,000,000 supply; the 1.5% floor arrives at year ≈10.30, first whole year 11).
27. **Associated token account finder** — the one canonical token account per (owner, mint): the PDA of the seeds [owner, token program, mint] under the Associated Token Account program (ATokenGPvbdGVxr1b2hvZbsiqW5xWH25efTNsLJA8knL), derived with the hub's full SHA-256 + ed25519 curve-check PDA engine; the classic SPL Token and Token-2022 programs give different ATAs for the same pair, stated on the page (headline: the hub's donation address holding the USDC mint under the classic program → BeJv9nhxhAhGVUpVaN4USa9TgpGiYhauc6VU5QyQZrbq, bump 255). Pure derivation — whether the account exists on-chain is not read.
28. **Compressed NFT tree planner** — the one on-chain account a cNFT collection pays for, sized from the SPL account-compression layout: 56 bytes of header + 64 fixed + a changelog ring of bufferSize × (40 + 32 × maxDepth) + 32 × maxDepth of rightmost proof + (2^(canopyDepth+1) − 2) × 32 of canopy, priced at its rent-exempt deposit (headline: depth 14, buffer 64, no canopy → a 31,800-byte account, 222,218,880 lamports for 16,384 assets, 31,232 of the bytes changelog). Buffer must be a power of two, stated on the page; the Bubblegum canopy minimum past depth 17 is flagged, and creation fees and per-mint costs are stated as not modelled.
29. **Seeded address finder (createWithSeed)** — Solana's other deterministic address: SHA-256(base ‖ seed ‖ owner), the derivation behind the system program's create_account_with_seed and the CLI's stake-account fan-out — no marker, no bump and no ed25519 curve check, so an on-curve result is valid, unlike a PDA (headline: the hub's donation address as base, seed "0", the Stake program as owner → 4z22cSCVREYe519iEet4xdqBSrTDx9muhtRR3fcRxpod, on-curve). The seed is a single string of at most 32 UTF-8 bytes, used byte-for-byte. Pure derivation — whether the account exists on-chain is not read.
30. **Token valuation calculator (market cap & FDV)** — the two valuations every token page quotes from one price: market cap = price × circulating supply, total valuation = price × total minted supply, FDV = price × max supply, each floored in exact BigInt, with the minted-but-not-circulating and not-yet-minted gaps valued as differences so the parts sum exactly to the FDV (headline: price 2.5, circulating 400,000,000, total 800,000,000, max 1,000,000,000 → market cap 1,000,000,000, FDV 2,500,000,000, a 2.5× multiple that is a pure supply ratio). Supplies must nest (circulating ≤ total ≤ max) or they are rejected. Price and supplies are user inputs — no live price or mint is read.
31. **Address lookup table planner** — the account behind v0 lookups: 56 bytes of metadata + 32 bytes per stored address (256 maximum, u8 indexes), priced at its rent-exempt deposit (64 addresses → a 2,104-byte account, 15,534,720 lamports), plus the per-transaction byte saving against inlining full keys — one loaded address costs 4 bytes more through a table, twenty save 585 bytes; the deactivation-cooldown reclaim is stated, not modelled.
32. **Oracle price confidence interpreter** — what a Pyth-style feed actually publishes: price ± conf at one exponent, conf being roughly one standard deviation, read as a band (150.00 ± 0.075 runs 149.925 to 150.075, just 5 bps wide) with its width in basis points of the price and the guard a consuming program applies — the textbook 67,890 ± 680 is 100.16 bps wide and fails a strict 100 bps guard. Exact BigInt on the raw feed integers; staleness is the feed's other check and is stated as not modelled, and no live feed is read.
33. **Bonding curve model (launchpad pricing)** — how a launchpad prices a token before any real pool exists: a constant-product curve over virtual reserves (price = virtual SOL ÷ virtual token), the virtual SOL a pricing offset while the real SOL raised is counted separately toward a graduation target. A buy pays the fee on the input first (an input — each venue sets its own), then runs Tool 2's swap math against the virtual reserves, so the quote agrees with the swap model to the smallest unit (headline: 30 virtual SOL / 1,000,000,000 virtual tokens, 42 of 85 SOL raised, a 3 SOL buy at 100 bps receives 90,081,892.62966333 tokens and lifts the price 20.78%; at the target the real SOL and unsold tokens migrate to an AMM and the virtual depth stays behind). Exact scaled-BigInt; reserves, target and fee are inputs and no live curve is read.
34. **Perpetual funding payment model** — what holding a perpetual future costs (or earns): payment = notional × rate × time, with the notional = size × mark price and the rate a signed input in basis points per hour — at a positive rate longs pay shorts, at a negative rate shorts pay longs — run in exact scaled BigInt and reported from the position's point of view (negative pays, positive receives), plus the annualised quoting figure (hourly rate × 8,760, a convention, not a forecast). Headline: a 100-unit long at 150 (a 15,000 notional) at 1 bp per hour pays 1.5 per hour and 36 over 24 hours. Every venue derives and caps its own rate and settles at its own interval, stated on the page; no live market is read.
35. **Lending interest-rate (kink) model** — how a Solana lending market prices a borrow off utilization (borrowed ÷ supplied): borrow rate = base + slope1 × (U ÷ Uopt) up to the optimal utilization, then base + slope1 + slope2 × (U − Uopt) ÷ (1 − Uopt) past it, all as exact BigInt rationals floored once, with the supply rate = borrow rate × U × (1 − reserve factor) and interest on the borrowed total compounded daily at a frozen utilization, stated as the model's convention (headline: 1,000,000 supplied / 800,000 borrowed sits exactly on the 80% kink — borrow rate 6%, supply rate 4.32%, interest 49,465.048542105 over 365 days; at 90% utilization the second slope lifts the borrow rate to 36%). Every curve parameter is an input — each market sets its own and governance changes them — and no live market is read.
36. **Perpetual position margin, PnL & liquidation model** — what a leveraged perpetual position actually risks: notional = size × entry, initial margin = notional ÷ leverage, unrealised PnL = (mark − entry) × size for a long (mirrored for a short), equity = margin + PnL, and a liquidation price solved exactly from equity = maintenance margin (a venue-set rate, an input, applied to the current notional): long (notional − margin) ÷ (size × (1 − mmr)), short (notional + margin) ÷ (size × (1 + mmr)) — so liquidation strikes while equity remains (headline: 100 units at 150, 10x, 500 bps maintenance posts 1,500 of margin, liquidates at 142.105263157, and shows a 3,000 PnL at a mark of 180). A long at leverage ≤ 1 has no liquidation price and is flagged, not priced at 0. Isolated margin only — cross margin, funding, fees and liquidation penalties are stated as not modelled — in exact scaled-BigInt; no live market is read.
37. **Stableswap swap model** — how a stable pool (Saber/Curve-style) prices two like-valued tokens off the stableswap invariant instead of x × y = k: D and the post-trade balance are solved by integer Newton iteration in exact BigInt (the on-chain method), the amplification coefficient A holds the price near 1:1 while the pool stays balanced and bends back toward constant-product pricing as it unbalances, and the swap fee is charged on the output (headline: a balanced 1,000,000 / 1,000,000 pool at A = 100 has D = 2,000,000 and returns a 10,000 trade at 9,999.502463058 gross against 9,900 on constant-product — leaving 9,995.502662073 after a 4 bps fee; unbalance it to 1,500,000 / 500,000 and the trade returns 9,910.453997586 gross). Two coins at the same decimals only — multi-coin pools and LP virtual-price growth are stated as not modelled — and no live pool is read.
38. **Lending liquidation calculator (maximum single liquidation)** — what one liquidation does to a position Tool 20 has judged liquidatable: the close factor caps the repay at debt × close factor, the liquidator seizes collateral worth the repay marked up by the liquidation bonus (seized = repay × (1 + bonus) ÷ price, the bonus being the liquidator's profit paid by the borrower), each step floored in exact BigInt, and if the marked-up seizure exceeds the collateral held the position is exhausted — the liquidator takes all of it, the effective repay falls to collateral value ÷ (1 + bonus), and the rest of the debt is left unbacked (headline: 10 at 100 against 500 of debt at a 500 bps bonus and 5,000 bps close factor repays 250 and seizes 2.625, a 12.5 bonus profit, leaving 250 of debt against 7.375 units; the underwater 2-unit case repays only 190.476190476 and leaves 309.523809524 unbacked). Bonus and close factor are inputs — every market sets its own, a 10,000 bps close factor represents a full close — single collateral and debt, no interest accrual, liquidation fee or sequencing, stated on the page.
39. **Two-hop swap route model** - what an aggregator's A -> M -> B route really returns: each hop is the hub's constant-product swap with its own fee on its own input, the full intermediate amount is swapped onward so the two fees and two price impacts compound, and the output is priced against the route's spot value (amount in x both pools' quoted prices, floored once in exact BigInt) to give the route's total cost in basis points (headline: 500,000 / 75,000,000 into 30,000,000 / 600,000 at 25 bps a hop turns 100 in into 14,959.515576642 of the intermediate and 298.293963175 out against a spot value of exactly 300; zero fees still return only 299.790146897, and a tenfold shallower second pool returns 296.965219193). Longer routes, split routes, CLMM hops and any interface fee on top are stated as not modelled.
40. **Exact-out swap model** — the fixed-output quote every wallet and payment flow needs: the pool's net requirement amountOut × reserveIn ÷ (reserveOut − amountOut), ceilinged at the smallest unit, grossed up to the smallest input whose floored fee withholding still leaves that net, with every reported figure taken from Tool 2's own swap at the solved input so the tools cannot disagree (headline: 1,000,000 / 1,000,000 at 25 bps delivers exactly 100,000 out for 111,389.585073796 in, a fee of 278.473962684; receiving 999 of a 1,000 / 1,000 pool costs 1,001,503.759398496 in, and a target at or beyond the whole output reserve is rejected — a constant-product pool can never pay it out). A flooring excess over the target is reported, never hidden; CLMM exact-out and interface fees are stated as not modelled.
41. **Arbitrage profit model** — what aligning a pool to an outside price earns: the swap leg is Tool 2's own swap, the output is valued at a user-quoted external price (input per output) floored once in exact BigInt, and the profit is that value minus the gross input, signed — against a 1,000,000 / 1,000,000 pool at an external price of 1.21, a 100,000 trade at no fee receives 90,909.09090909 worth 109,999.999999998 (a profit of 9,999.999999998 and a post-trade spot of 1.209999999), doubling the trade collapses the profit to 1,666.666666665 as the pool's price overshoots, and at 25 bps the profit is 9,749.943168901 with the spot stopping at 1.209450062. The external sale is assumed to fill in full at the quoted price — external fees, slippage and transaction costs are stated as not modelled — and no live price or pool is read.
42. **CLMM swap model** — a swap inside one tick range (Raydium CLMM, Orca Whirlpools): paying token0 sets 1/√P_new = 1/√P_now + effectiveIn ÷ L and pays L × (√P_now − √P_new) of token1, paying token1 mirrors it, with the fee withheld from the gross input first in exact scaled-BigInt and the square-root-price math in floating point like Tool 17 (on-chain it is Q64.64 fixed point). Headline: L = 1,000,000 over ticks −1,000…1,000 at tick 0, paying 10,000 token0 at no fee returns 9,900.99009901 and moves the price to 0.9802960494; at 25 bps it returns 9,876.482091141 — exactly Tool 2's output for a 1,000,000 / 1,000,000 pool, because inside one range the swap is a constant-product swap on the range's virtual reserves. In a −100…100 range the same trade reaches the bound after 5,012.269623051 of effective input, paying 4,987.272070749 — the unused remainder and the tick crossing it would need on-chain are reported, not modelled, and no live pool is read.
43. **Limit-order book walk** - what a market buy on a central limit order book (Phoenix, OpenBook, Manifest) actually pays: it fills against resting asks in price order at each level's own price, each level's cost floored at the smallest unit in exact scaled-BigInt, reported as filled quantity, total cost, volume-weighted average price (VWAP), the extra cost over filling entirely at the best ask, and the unfilled remainder when the book runs out (headline: asks of 50 at 100, 100 at 101 and 200 at 103 fill a buy of 120 for 12,070 at a VWAP of 100.583333333, exactly 70 over the best-ask cost; a buy of 1,000 fills only the book's full 350 depth for 35,700 and leaves 650 unfilled). Three ask levels modelled; time priority, deeper levels and venue taker fees are stated as not modelled, and no live book is read.
44. **TWAP oracle calculator** — the time-weighted average price a pool's oracle reports: the pool adds its tick to a signed tick-cumulative counter every second, and the TWAP over a window is 1.0001^((cumulativeEnd − cumulativeStart) ÷ seconds), the division truncating toward zero in exact BigInt with the discarded remainder reported (headline: a cumulative moving 0 → 360,000 over 3,600 seconds averages tick 100, a TWAP of 1.010049662, sitting 97.55 bps below a spot of 1.02 — the lag that makes a TWAP expensive to manipulate and slow to follow a real move; a −100 change over 3 seconds averages tick −33, not the floor's −34). Cumulatives and spot are user inputs — no live pool or oracle is read.
45. **LP withdrawal calculator** — what burning LP tokens returns from a constant-product pool: burn ÷ supply of the pool's CURRENT reserves per token, each floored at the smallest unit in exact scaled-BigInt, with the remaining pool reported (headline: reserves of 2,000,000 / 500,000 against a supply of 1,000,000 return 500,000 / 125,000 for a burn of 250,000 — a 25% share of what the pool holds now, not a return of any original deposit). A full burn returns the entire reserves exactly and leaves the pool empty; a dust burn against a large supply can floor to zero on a side, flagged rather than hidden. Reserves and supply are user inputs — no live pool is read.
46. **Airdrop / multisend cost planner** — the SOL a token distribution needs before any token moves: a rent-exempt deposit of 2,039,280 lamports for each recipient who needs a new token account (Tool 3's figure for 165 bytes, composed so the tools cannot disagree — locked, not spent, and reclaimable when an account closes), plus batched-transaction fees at Tool 5's 5,000-lamport base per signature plus a user-chosen priority fee per transaction, with the batch size a user input bounded by the packet limit rather than a pinned constant (headline: 1,000 recipients, 800 new accounts, batches of 25 and 100 tokens each take 40 transactions and 1.631624 SOL to fund, plus 100,000 tokens). Failed transfers, retries and distributor-program fees are stated as not modelled; no live cluster is read.
47. **Dutch auction price calculator** — the price of a decaying sale right now: start − (start − floor) × elapsed ÷ duration in exact scaled-BigInt, floored at the smallest unit, with the ends exact (the start at t = 0, the floor the moment the duration runs — a step that does not divide the duration is cut short at the end). An optional step turns the continuous fall into a stepped one, the form many on-chain sales use (headline: 10 down to 1 over 3,600 seconds stands at 7.75 after 900 seconds and 5.5 halfway; in 600-second steps it holds 8.5 until 1,200 seconds, then steps to 7). Exponential decay is stated as a different, unmodelled form; no live sale is read.
48. **Governance quorum & approval calculator** — whether a DAO vote actually passed, judged on both bars: quorum (votes cast — for + against + abstain — reaching a ceilinged share of the governing supply) and approval (for ÷ (for + against), floored to a whole basis point, reaching the threshold), with abstentions counting toward quorum and toward neither side of approval (headline: 600,000 for / 200,000 against / 100,000 abstaining on a 10,000,000 supply at a 10% quorum and 60% threshold wins the for-share 75% yet FAILS, 100,000 short of quorum; 700,000 for passes at exactly 1,000,000 cast and 77.77%). A cast above the supply is rejected; no live proposal is read.
49. **Token supply allocation planner** — splitting a fixed supply into launch buckets: the named shares (liquidity, community, team) are priced in basis points and floored at the smallest unit in exact scaled-BigInt, and the treasury, the designated remainder bucket, receives the supply minus the floored amounts, so the parts sum to the supply exactly and the flooring dust (provably 0 to 3 smallest units, zero at whole-token supplies) has a declared home — its nominal share and the dust are reported separately (headline: 1,000,000,000 at 40% / 25% / 20% splits 400,000,000 / 250,000,000 / 200,000,000 with a 150,000,000 treasury remainder; a supply of 1.000000001 at 3,333 bps a bucket leaves the treasury 1 smallest unit of dust). Shares summing past 10,000 bps are rejected; no live token is read.
51. **Token burn & holder-share calculator** — what destroying tokens does to the shares that remain: the supply falls by the burned amount and a holder who does not burn rises from balance ÷ supply to balance ÷ (supply − burn), floored to whole basis points in exact BigInt, with their balance unchanged (headline: burning 100,000,000 of 1,000,000,000 leaves 900,000,000 and lifts a 10,000,000 holder from 1% to 1.11%). The burn is of other holders' tokens, so it is bounded by supply − holder and burning all of that leaves the holder at exactly 100%; a burn does not by itself set a price and none is modelled. No live token is read.
52. **Merkle airdrop proof planner** — how an airdrop or allowlist commits N recipients to one 32-byte root stored on-chain: the tree completes to the next power of two at or above N, depth is the smallest d with 2^d ≥ N, and each claim supplies a proof of one 32-byte sibling hash per level (headline: 10,000 recipients → depth 14, 16,384 padded leaves, 32,767 nodes, a 448-byte proof per claim; 16,384 still fits depth 14 while recipient 16,385 pushes every proof to 480 bytes, and a single recipient's proof is empty). Exact BigInt counts; leaf contents, hash function and claim program are not modelled, and no live tree is read.
53. **Multisig approval planner** — how an M-of-N multisig (the Squads form on Solana) balances safety against liveness: approvals still needed, the share of members the threshold represents, and the two exact subset counts — the member subsets large enough to approve, sum_{k=M}^{N} C(N,k) of 2^N (headline: a 3-of-5 at 60% has 16 of 32 approving subsets), and the smallest blocking coalition of N − M + 1 refusers (any 3 freeze a 3-of-5; a unanimous threshold hands every member a veto). A 64-address table deactivated 600 slots ago with only 450 blocks produced (150 skipped) still has 62 blocks to wait, even though the earliest possible close slot (deactivation + 512) has passed; closing then reclaims the full 15,534,720 lamports (0.01553472 SOL) of the 2,104-byte account, composed from Tool 31's own layout. A frozen table can never be closed; no live table, slot or height is read.
95. **Stake split planner** - what dividing one stake account into new ones costs: each new 200-byte account sets aside its own 2,282,880-lamport rent-exempt reserve before any of its share is delegated (Tool 83's funding rule per account), so splitting 30 SOL out of a 100 SOL account into three accounts delegates 9.99771712 SOL per account and parks 0.00684864 SOL of extra reserves outside delegation; a full drain is cheaper by one reserve because the source closes. Split shares divide as evenly as lamports allow and sum to the split total exactly; no live account is read.
96. **Stake merge planner** - the split run backwards: merging a source stake account into a destination empties and closes the source, lamports are conserved exactly, and the pair's two parked 2,282,880-lamport reserves become one, so merging always frees exactly one reserve (0.00228288 SOL) back into delegation whatever the balances - a 40 SOL source merged into a 60 SOL destination lifts delegation from 99.99543424 SOL to 99.99771712 SOL, and two reserve-only accounts merge into an account delegating exactly one reserve. Only a compatible pair merges on-chain (same authorities and lockup, compatible delegation states); no live account is read.
97. **Launchpad oversubscription allocator** - the pro-rata rule of a fixed-price sale: when the total committed passes the hard cap, every buyer is allocated cap x contribution / total committed (floored at the smallest unit) and refunded the rest, and tokens follow the filled part only - a 100 SOL cap with 400 SOL committed fills a 10 SOL contribution at 2.5 SOL, refunds 7.5 SOL and delivers 25,000 of a 1,000,000 token supply; at or under the cap everyone fills in full and the unsold tokens stay with the sale. Individual caps, whitelists, tiers and launchpad fees are not modelled; no live sale is read.
98. **Order-book tick & lot planner** - the grid a central limit order book rests orders on: prices only at whole ticks (a buy rounds down, a sell rounds up) and sizes only at whole lots (quantity rounds down, the remainder is dust) - a buy named at 150.37 on a 0.10 tick rests at 150.30, and 10.37 on a 0.25 lot trades 10.25 with 0.12 of dust, a notional of 1,540.575 against 1,559.3369 intended. A quantity below one lot cannot be placed; venue fees are not modelled; no live market is read.
100. **Single-sided (zap) deposit planner** - why the right swap is not half: a zap-in swaps part of a one-sided holding to the other token, then deposits in the pool's ratio, and the optimum is the floored root of a quadratic in the swap size - the swap moves the price the unswapped half is measured against. At 1,000,000 / 4,000,000 with 10,000 in at 25 bps it swaps 4,993.804386958, deposits 5,006.19561304 with all 19,826.517279367 received and strands 0.000000002, while swapping half strands 49.256819176 outside the position; even at zero fee the root is 4,987.562112089. Slippage protection and interface zap fees are stated as not modelled; no live pool is read.
107. **Perp insurance fund & socialized-loss planner** - who pays when a liquidation falls short of what the winning side is owed: the pre-funded insurance fund pays first (min(shortfall, fund)), the uncovered rest is haircut from the winning side's profits pro-rata and floored per winner at the smallest unit, and anything beyond every winner's profit combined stays as bad debt - a 50,000 shortfall against a 20,000 fund and 200,000 of profits pays 20,000 from the fund, haircuts 30,000 (1,500 bps), and leaves a 10,000 winner receiving 8,500, while 30,000 uncovered against 20,000 of profits leaves 10,000 of bad debt. Ranked ADL, venue caps and fund replenishment are not modelled; no live venue is read.
106. **Quadratic funding matching planner** - how a grant round's matching pool actually splits: a project's raw match is (sum of the square roots of its contributions) squared minus what it raised, so breadth beats size - 100 and 25 from two funders match 100, a lone 400 matches exactly zero, and four 9s match 108; a pool that covers the raw total pays in full and leaves the rest undistributed (1,000 leaves 792 of 208), and a short pool scales every match pro-rata, floored (104 pays 50 / 0 / 54, 100 pays 48.076923076 / 51.923076923 and leaves one smallest unit of dust). Square roots are floored at the smallest unit; identity checks against split-wallet gaming, per-round caps and platform rounding are not modelled; no live round is read.
105. **Flash loan repayment & profit planner** - what borrowing inside one transaction actually costs: the loan must return the amount borrowed plus its fee in the same transaction or the whole transaction reverts, the fee is ceilinged at the smallest unit so a one-smallest-unit borrow at 1 bp pays a full unit (an effective 10,000 bps), and proceeds are judged against the repayment - borrowing 10,000 at 30 bps repays 10,030, proceeds of 10,050 profit 20 (20 bps on the borrow) and still repay any fee up to 50 bps, while proceeds of 10,010 cover the principal but fall 20 short of the fee and proceeds below the borrow fail at every rate. What the borrowed funds do mid-transaction is entered as the proceeds figure; the SOL transaction fee is not modelled; no live loan or pool is read.
104. **Single-sided (zap) exit planner** - leaving a constant-product pool into one token, and what consolidating costs: burn LP tokens and take Tool 45's pro-rata pair, then swap all of the token B received back into token A through Tool 2's swap against the post-withdrawal reserves, both legs composed verbatim; burning 100,000 LP of a 1,000,000 supply against a balanced 1,000,000 / 1,000,000 pool at a 25 bps fee withdraws 100,000 of each token and ends with 189,797.44936234 A - a consolidation cost of 10,202.55063766 A (510 bps) against the pair's 200,000 A value at the post-withdrawal spot, exactly 500 bps at a zero fee and 2,503 bps on a 50% exit - while burning the whole supply leaves no pool to swap into and no single-sided total, and a dust burn whose B side floors to zero costs nothing. Slippage protection and interface zap fees are stated as not modelled; the single-sided entry is Tool 100; no live pool is read.
103. **Second-price (Vickrey) auction settlement planner** - what winning a sealed-bid auction actually costs: the highest bid wins but pays the larger of the second-highest bid and the reserve, so a 120 high bid against a 90 second bid pays 90 (a saving of 30, 2,500 bps of the bid) and settles through the sale split - 1.8 to the platform at 200 bps, 4.5 to the creator at 500 bps, 83.7 to the seller - while a second bid of 40 against a 50 reserve still pays the 50 reserve, a tie at the top pays the full tied amount, a highest bid below the reserve sells nothing and charges nobody, and a lone bid with no rival and no reserve pays exactly zero, the degenerate case a reserve exists to prevent. Increment rules and proxy / autobid ladders are stated as not modelled; the English (open) form is Tool 102; no live auction is read.
102. **Auction minimum-increment & settlement planner** - the least bid that wins an English auction, and what winning pays: the next bid must clear the current bid plus a minimum increment (a percentage of the current bid, ceilinged at the smallest unit, so the minimum always strictly exceeds the current bid - on a 0.000000001 bid even a 1 bp increment makes the minimum 0.000000002) and any reserve; at a current bid of 100, a 50 reserve and a 500 bps increment the minimum is 105, a bid of 120 wins (2,000 bps above the current bid, next minimum 126) and settles first-price through the sale split - 2.4 to the platform at 200 bps, 6 to the creator at 500 bps, 111.6 to the seller - while a bid of 104 falls 1 short and is charged nothing. Fixed increments and anti-sniping extensions are stated as not modelled; the second-price form is Tool 103; no live auction is read.
101. **Pool creation planner** - the opening price, the first mint and the locked minimum: the two seed deposits set a new pool's opening price (10,000 A with 40,000 B opens at 4 B per A), the first mint is the geometric mean of the seeds by exact integer square root (20,000 LP), and a pool-specific minimum slice is permanently locked as an inflation-attack defence - at a 1 LP lock the creator receives 19,999 LP (9,999 bps), the locked slice claims 0.5 A and 2 B that can never be redeemed, and redeeming all the creator's LP at once would return 9,999.5 A and 39,998 B. Pool creation fees and account rent are stated as not modelled; no live pool is read.
99. **Vault share planner** - how a share vault prices a deposit, and how a donation breaks it: shares mint at deposit x shares / assets, floored in raw smallest units, a transfer in raises the price per share without minting any, and the rounding loss lands on the depositor - at 1,000 assets / 1,000 shares with a 9,000 donation, a 5,005 deposit mints 500 shares and redeems 5,001 (4 lost to the flooring; an exact 5,000 loses nothing), while 1 share outstanding against a 999,999 donation makes a 500,000 deposit mint zero shares and lose everything. Vault defences (locked seeds, virtual share offsets, caps) are stated as not modelled; no live vault is read.
81. **Priority fee percentile planner** - which price of a recent sample a bid actually beats: the fee market is local to the accounts a transaction writes, so estimators sample recent prioritization fees for those accounts and bid a nearest-rank percentile of the sample; with 40 samples at 0, 30 at 1,000, 20 at 10,000 and 10 at 100,000 micro-lamports per compute unit, the 75th percentile is rank 75 of 100 and lands on 10,000 micro-lamports (90 samples, 9,000 bps, at or below it), paying 2,000 lamports at a 200,000 compute-unit limit against the sample median's 200 - a difference of 1,800 lamports. The RPC's own recent-fee values are per-slot minimums, a floor rather than a distribution; samples are user-supplied and no live fee data is read.
